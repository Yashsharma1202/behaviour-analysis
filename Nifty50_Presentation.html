01
The idea

Trade the calendar, not a hunch.

Corporate events — quarterly results, board meetings, dividends, announcements — have dates that are public in advance. Markets anticipate them on the way in and digest them on the way out.

So we test one disciplined rule: buy N days before a known event, hold through it, and sell M days after — and measure how often that actually made money.

We never forecast the event.

The date is read off the regulatory calendar. The only thing we study is the price behaviour around it.

03
How it works

Brute-force honesty.

For each event we test every combination of “buy N days before” × “sell M days after” (N,M = 1…8) — an 8 × 8 = 64-rule grid — against the stock’s full history, and keep the best.

return% = ( close[T+M] / close[T−N] − 1 ) × 100
WIN if > 0   // per event

win rate = wins ÷ total events × 100

no machine learning   Just exhaustive search + transparent statistics — every number traces to one formula.

07
The honest bottom line

What the numbers really say.

Past win-rates around events are real — but they are in-sample (a rule chosen on the same history it’s scored on) and modest out of sample.

No robust, reliably tradeable edge survives out-of-sample.

Even a pooled, time-validated ML test on 6,000 events landed at ~coin-flip.

That is itself a valuable, defensible result — and it’s presented transparently, not hidden.

The real deliverable

A transparent framework and a clean, verified, fully-historical Nifty 50 dataset — a solid base for deeper, more careful research.
