Technical Study Report

Event-Driven Earnings Drift Model (Long vs Short)

This technical study documents the methodology, mathematics, and operational logic of the Event-Driven Earnings Drift Model implemented on ports 8080 (Long-Only) and 8000 (Short-Selling).

1. Executive Summary & Strategy Core

The core methodology is built on the Earnings Drift Anomaly positioning. Instead of predicting the direction or surprise of a company's upcoming earnings report, the model positions around the Catalyst Announcement Date (Result Date).

By analyzing a stock's historical price behavior around its past earnings announcements, the engine optimizes entry and exit offsets to capture:

  • The Pre-Result Anticipation Drift ("Buy the Rumour" run-up or "Sell the Rumour" profit booking).
  • The Post-Result Digest Drift / PEAD (Post-Earnings Announcement Drift as the market digests the surprise).

2. Leg Mechanics & Mathematical Formulation

Every event-driven trade is executed as a two-leg transaction matching specific trading-day offsets relative to the Result Announcement Date (T).

A. Long Strategy (Buy Before, Sell After)

Used when a stock exhibits consistent upward momentum leading into or immediately following results.

  • Entry (Buy): Executed at the Closing Price on trading day T − N.
  • Exit (Sell): Executed at the Closing Price on trading day T + M.

Math Formulation:

Long Return (%) = [ ( Price_exit / Price_entry ) - 1 - Cost ] * 100

Where Cost represents transaction costs / slippage fraction (default: 0.10% or 0.0010 round-trip).

B. Short Strategy (Sell Before, Buy After)

Used when a stock exhibits consistent downward momentum or pre-result profit booking.

  • Entry (Short Sell): Executed at the Closing Price on trading day T − N.
  • Exit (Cover Buy): Executed at the Closing Price on trading day T + M.

Math Formulation:

Short Return (%) = [ ( Price_entry - Price_exit ) / Price_entry - Cost ] * 100

3. How the Engine Predicts the Candle (Grid Optimization)

The model determines the optimal buy and sell offsets (N and M) using ex-ante historical grid backtesting over the past quarters:

┌──────────────────────────────┐ │ Historical Event Dates │ └──────────────┬───────────────┘ │ [For each (N, M) combination] Long: N,M ∈ [1, 8] │ Short: N ∈ [1, 10], M ∈ [1, 15] ▼ ┌──────────────────────────────┐ │ Simulate Trade Returns │ └──────────────┬───────────────┘ │ ▼ ┌──────────────────────────────┐ │ Calculate Metrics per Stock: │ │ - Average Return (%) │ │ - Win Rate (%) │ └──────────────┬───────────────┘ │ ▼ ┌──────────────────────────────┐ │ Pick optimal parameters. │ │ (Max Win Rate > Max Return) │ └──────────────────────────────┘
  1. Parameters Grid Search:
    • Long-Only Grid: Entry Offset (N) from 1 to 8 days, Exit Offset (M) from 1 to 8 days (8x8 grid).
    • Short-Selling Grid: Entry Offset (N) from 1 to 8 days, Exit Offset (M) from 1 to 8 days (8x8 grid).
  2. Backtesting Matrix: The engine evaluates a symmetric 64 combinations (8x8) for both Long and Short strategies across historical earnings result dates to prevent overfitting.
  3. Objective Optimization Function: The optimal offset combination is chosen by ranking combinations first by Win Rate (%), and then by Average Return (%).

4. Port 8080 (Long-Only) vs Port 8000 (Short-Selling) Comparison

Dimension Port 8080 (Long-Only) Port 8000 (Short-Selling)
Operational Logic Buy first at T-N, sell later at T+M. Sell first at T-N, buy back (cover) at T+M.
Market View Bullish anticipation / post-result reaction. Bearish anticipation / negative earnings drift.
Profit Trigger Exit Price > Entry Price Exit Price < Entry Price

5. Summary Matrix (Losing Trades Decision Logic)

Our backtest highlights that having a combined strategy selection model is optimal. Even when trades result in a loss, the decision matrix selects the leg that minimizes losses. For example:

  • NTPC (Q2): Choosing SHORT resulted in a -0.45% loss, avoiding the -1.18% loss from the LONG strategy.
  • SHRIRAMFIN (Q2): Choosing LONG resulted in a -1.49% loss, avoiding the massive -12.32% loss from the SHORT strategy.
