2.1 The trade mechanics

  • Buy before: buy the stock future at the close of the N-th trading day before Laxmi Pujan.
  • Hold through: carry the position over the Muhurat session and the closed days.
  • Sell after: sell at the close of the M-th trading day after it. Return = (Pexit / Pentry − 1) × direction; all current positions are LONG.
  • There is no stop-loss inside the backtest. The −3.5% spot stop in the playbook is a risk control suggested for live trading and is not in the numbers.

3.3 What this step showed

  • The basket was up in 20 of 26 years with one common window. The down years were 2009, 2012, 2013, 2015, 2016, 2018.
  • Individual stocks differ a lot: a stock’s own best window wins far more often in-sample than the common window (79.4% vs 60.7% of trades) — but part of that is the search itself, which is why step 3 exists.
  • All trades in one year share the same market backdrop, so the 759 trades of Final A are really 26 observations of the market, not 759.

4. Step 2: what drives the result

4.1 LONG versus SHORT

For each stock the best LONG window was compared with its best SHORT window on the 8×8 grid (≥ 7 valid years). Nifty 50: LONG is better for 45 of 47 stocks, SHORT for 1, with 1 exact tie (ties default to LONG in the scan). Across the 135 tested non-Nifty F&O stocks LONG is better for 128, SHORT for 1, and 6 are ties (see Diwali_Positions_8x8_Nifty_FO.xlsx). Average best-window win rate on the Nifty: LONG 77.7% vs SHORT 53.6%. Part of the LONG bias is general upward drift in prices; part is Diwali.

4.2 Which windows work: the 8 × 8 grid for the Nifty-50 basket (all years)

Rows = entry N days before T, columns = exit M days after T. Left: average LONG return of the equal-weight basket. Right: share of the 26 years in which the basket was up.

On the 8×8 grid the basket's average return is positive in 64 of 64 windows and the up-year share is at least 60% in 64 of 64. Entering 5–8 days before and leaving 3–8 days after averages +2.75%, against +2.25% for entering only 1–2 days before; the profitable zone is wide, so the result does not hinge on one exact window.

4.8 Conclusions that went into the final design

  • Direction: LONG only; no SHORT passed the evidence bar.
  • Entry: early entries (T-6 to T-8) average +4.13% per trade against +2.71% for late entries (T-1 to T-2); see table 4.3.
  • Per-stock windows beat one common window in-sample, but only a part of the gain is real — hence the conviction filter.
  • One event a year: size the book as a single yearly bet; a bad cycle hits every position together.

5. Step 3: choosing the windows and checking them

5.1 How the book was chosen (protection against curve-fitting)

  • Minimum history: a stock needs ≥ 7 valid Diwali years in the chosen window; three Nifty stocks failed this and are left out of the main book.
  • Walk-forward: for each year from 2007, the best window is re-picked using only earlier years and traded in that year. This is shown next to every in-sample pick.
  • Conviction gate: only STRONG and MODERATE stocks are in the book. NO TRADE stocks (best in-sample win rate but walk-forward below the bar) are excluded.
  • Grid: the project's 8×8 grid (N, M = 1..8) is used for every stock, so the earliest 2026 entry is T-8 = 28-Oct and no re-pick for passed entries is needed.
  • Honest limit: 128 combinations are scanned per stock, so the best in-sample win rate is optimistic by construction.

6. Final Strategy A: the 34-stock Diwali book

Buy the stock future of each of the 34 names at the close of its entry day (T-N) and sell it at the close of T+M. All positions LONG, one lot each, entries from 28-Oct-2026 to 06-Nov-2026, exits from 09-Nov to 19-Nov-2026. Laxmi Pujan / Muhurat is Sunday 08-Nov-2026; 10-Nov (Balipratipada) is a market holiday. Planned margin ≈ ₹46.46 L (20% of contract value).

8. Assumptions and limitations

  • Few independent observations: 26 Diwali events. The 759 trades of Final A all share each year's market backdrop; effective sample size is nearer 26 than 759.
  • Chosen by search: 128 combinations per stock, picked on the same years they are scored on. Even with walk-forward and a conviction gate, a searched rule usually does worse live than in the backtest — the out-of-sample rows are the better guide.
  • Look-ahead in the stock list: which 34 stocks are in the book was decided using full-history evidence; the ‘all 50’ out-of-sample rows have no such filter.
  • Daily closes only: entries and exits are at closes, not 09:20 / 15:15 intraday fills; real slippage, especially on stocks that move into the close, is not modelled.
  • P&L scale: ₹ P&L uses today’s lot sizes and prices for every year, so it is a consistent weight, not what a trader would have earned at that year's prices. This is different from the earlier Diwali_Holiday_Performance_Report, which used raw prices of 2022–2025; its ₹ figures will not match.
  • Costs and margin: 0.15% round trip and 20% margin are assumptions; actual SPAN + exposure and brokerage vary.
  • Universe bias: only stocks that are in the Nifty 50 / F&O list today; stocks that dropped out earlier are not tested.
  • Calendar: the Laxmi Pujan dates 2000–2025 were hand-corrected and verified against price data; the 2026 holiday list assumes 10-Nov is closed. Re-check before trading.
  • Grid change: this report uses the project's 8×8 grid. The earlier Diwali_Final_Strategy_Report and Diwali_Holiday_Performance_Report used a 10×10 scan and are superseded.
  • Gap risk: a long future can lose more than any stop; there is no stop in the backtest.
